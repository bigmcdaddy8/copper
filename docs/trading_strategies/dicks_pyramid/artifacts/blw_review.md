# Mechanics of Pyramiding Trend-Following
To understand the exact mechanics of a pyramiding trend-following strategy, we need to move past theory and look at concrete rules. Adding to a winning position (pyramiding) increases your potential payout exponentially, but if done incorrectly, a minor pullback will instantly wipe out your profits because your average entry price rises.
To prevent this, systematic trend followers use strict mathematical triggers. Below are two primary mechanical frameworks used to scale into trades while safely trailing the Stop Loss (SL).
------------------------------
## Framework 1: The "ATR Brick" Method (Volatility-Based)
This is a standard mechanical approach used by professional trend followers. It uses the Average True Range (ATR) to measure the market's current volatility and dictates exactly when to add size and move stops.
Assume you are Longing an Asset at $100, and the current 14-day ATR is $2. You decide a "brick" (one unit of position size) will be added every 1 ATR ($2) move in your favor.

| Position State | Entry Price | Current SL | Logic / Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial Entry (1 Unit) | $100.00 | $96.00 | Initial stop is set at 2 ATR below entry ($100 - $4). Risk is defined. |
| Add Unit #2 (2 Units Total) | $102.00 | $98.00 | Price moved up 1 ATR ($2). SL is trailed up by exactly $2. Total risk remains locked at the initial dollar amount. |
| Add Unit #3 (3 Units Total) | $104.00 | $100.00 | Price moved up another ATR. SL is trailed to breakeven ($100). The initial trade is now completely risk-free. |
| Add Unit #4 (4 Units Total) | $106.00 | $102.00 | Final unit added. SL trailed to $102. Your worst-case scenario is now a guaranteed profit on the overall position. |

## Key Rules for the ATR Method:

* When to move the SL: Every single time a new unit is added, the SL for the entire combined position moves up by the exact distance of the trigger (1 ATR).
* When to stop adding: Most professional systems cap a pyramid at 4 total units. Adding more than that raises your average entry price too close to the current market price, making you highly vulnerable to a sharp reversal.

------------------------------
## Framework 2: The "Breakout & Pullback" Method (Price Action-Based)
If you prefer looking at market structure rather than pure math, this method utilizes visual support and resistance levels. You add to your position when the market proves the trend is continuing by breaking old highs.

      (Add Unit 3 & Trail SL to Support 2) ->   [New High]
                                                  /
                    [Support 2] ---------------- / (Pullback)
                        /
      (Add Unit 2 & Trail SL to Support 1) ->   [Old High]
                                                  /
                    [Support 1] ---------------- / (Pullback)
                        /
  (Initial Entry & SL below Support 0) ->     [Base]

## The Mechanics:

   1. Initial Entry: You enter 1 unit on a breakout from a base. Your SL is placed just below the recent swing low (Support 0).
   2. The Waiting Phase: The market rallies, tops out, and pulls back to form a higher low (Support 1). You do nothing yet.
   3. The Trigger (Add Unit 2): When the price rallies again and breaks above the previous high, you buy Unit 2.
   4. The SL Adjustment: The moment Unit 2 is triggered, the SL for both Unit 1 and Unit 2 is moved up to just below the newly established Support 1.
   5. Repeat: You repeat this cycle at Support 2 on the next breakout.

------------------------------
## The Unbreakable Math: The Pyramid Scale-Down
To make pyramiding work safely, you must follow the Golden Rule of Trend Following: Never add more size than your previous entry. Your position structure should look like a pyramid, not an inverted triangle.

* Correct (Pyramid): Entry 1 (4 Lots) → Entry 2 (2 Lots) → Entry 3 (1 Lot).
* Why: Your average entry price stays heavily weighted toward the bottom, far away from the current market price.
* Incorrect (Inverted): Entry 1 (1 Lot) → Entry 2 (2 Lots) → Entry 3 (4 Lots).
* Why: A tiny 1% pullback will plunge the entire trade into a massive loss because your biggest position size was bought at the absolute highest price.
